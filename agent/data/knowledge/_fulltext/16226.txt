Summary
In the face of the challenges of global climate change and sustainable development, the energy
consumption and environmental impact of high performance computing ( HPC ) technology have attracted
increasing attention. The purpose of this study is to quantify the annual energy consumption of HPC
systems by establishing a compre hensive model, evaluate their carbon emissions and environmental
impacts, and predict the development trend in 2030, so as to provide strategies and suggestions for the
green development of HPC.
For Task I, we first collected and analyzed the energy consumption data of HPC systems in the
United States and China from 2015 to 2023, and found that there were significant differences in the annual
energy consumption of HPC systems under full load and average utilization conditions. Then we calculate
the total energy consumption of the global HPC syst em, and define the problem under two conditions : full
load operation and average utilization rate, which p rovides accurate basic data for subsequent carbon
emission modeling.
For Task II, we give different energy structures of HPC e nergy consumption, including fossil fuels
and renewable energy. Then a progressive model is designed : Firstly, an energy structure model is
established to describe the distribution and sour ces of different energy sources in the total energy
consumption. Then a carbon emission accounting model is established to calculate the carbon emissions of
each energy consumption separately. Finally, the environmental impact model is established and the EII
index is introduced to quantify the comb ined impact of carbon emissions on climate, ecology and air quality.
For Task III, starting from the growth trend of HPC, we firs t establish a prediction model to evaluate
future energy demand, and calculate the total carbon emissions in 2030 by combining dynamic energy
structure and carbon emission coefficient. Then, b y introducing the environmental impact model, the
comprehensive impact of different energy consumption on climate, ecology and air quality is quantified, and
the prediction range under optimistic and pessimistic scenarios is further explored. In addition, our team also
considered the potential impact of energy technology development and policy changes, and used HPC in
Task I and Task II for simulation verification. The result is that the combination of high-growth HPC
and traditional fossil fuel structure in the first sce nario is expected to lead to a significant increase in
carbon emissions by 2030, while the EII index is also growing. Under the second scenario, the annual
growth rate of carbon emissions will decrease, but th e overall emissions may still be high, but the EII
index will decrease.
For Task IV, we extend the model by increasing the analysis of the proportion of renewable energy
use and taking into account the impact of water consumption. We found that increasing the proportion of
renewable energy and optimizing water resources management can further reduce the environmental
impact of HPC. In addition, we propose an extended environmental impact index ( EII ) model to
assess the environmental impact of HPC more comprehensively.
Finally, based on the model results, we propose a series of technical and policy recommendations to
reduce the environmental impact of HPC. The simulation results of implementing these recommendations
show that the carbon emissions of HPC can be significantly reduced by optimizing the energy structure and
improving energy efficiency. We drafted a lette r to the UN Advisory Committee recommending the
inclusion of the environmental impact of HPC in the 2030 Development Goals.
Keywords: HPC, Energy Structure, Carbon Emission Accounting, Environmental Impact, EII

High performance computing ( HPC )
technology is developing at an alarming rate and is
widely used in artificial intelligence, data science

expansion of this technology has also caused
increasingly serious environmental problems. The
large-scale operation of HPC systems relies on high-
energy-consuming data centers and advanced

demand, but also has a profound impact on global
carbon emissions and resource sustainability

Especially in areas where electricity is dominated by
fossil fuels, the contribution of HPC energy
consumption to climate change is particularly
prominent

In addition to energy issues, the environmental i mpact of HPC also involves multiple dimensions. For
example, the large demand for cooling water in data centers may lead to water shortage and pollution risks.

location and construction of data centers may lead to land use conflicts and ecosystem destruction
[4].T h e s e
complex environmental problems make it imperative t o assess the full life cycle environmental impact of
HPC, and also put forward higher requirements on how to achieve the balance between technology and
sustainable development goals
[5].
1.2 Literature Review
In the current context of global climate change and s ustainable development, the construction and
operation of energy big data centers have become a key link in achieving the "dual carbon" goals (carbon
peak and carbon neutrality). Sun, Wang, and Ai
[6] explored the impact mechanism of big data development
on carbon emissions in Chinese counties through empirical research, providing a theoretical basis for carbon
emission reduction at the county level. Chang, Jin, and Xue [7] analyzed the impact of big data strategy on
carbon emissions from electricity consumption fro m the quasi-natural experiment of national big data
comprehensive pilot zones, providing empirical s upport for the application of big data technology in the
energy field. Yang [8] analyzed the impact of carbon peak and carbon neutrality on data centers, presenting
potential challenges and opportunities for data centers in achieving carbon emission reduction targets. Mao
et al. [9] studied the "Green Energy Code" system based on energy big data centers, exploring the system's
potential in promoting energy consumptio n and carbon emission monitoring. Shuai [10] discussed the
combination of energy big data and carbon emission monitoring from the perspective of enterprise
management, providing new ideas for corporat e carbon emission management. Yang and Yuan
[11] discussed
the construction of Zhangjiakou Energy Big Data C enter under the "dual carbon" background, emphasizing
the center's important role in promoting regional energy transition and improving energy efficiency. Bu [12]
analyzed the problems and countermeasures in the cons truction of energy big dat a centers driven by "carbon
neutrality," providing strategic guidance for the low -carbon development of data centers. Huang, Zhao, and
Yang[13] conducted an in-depth study on the carbon emission accounting methods of data centers under the
"dual carbon" goal and demonstrated the application effects of the accounting methods through case analysis.
The unveiling of the Qinghai Province Smart Dual Carbon Big Data Center [14] marks the local government's
active exploration in promoting the integration of energy big data and carbon emission management. Zhu et
al.
[15] studied the impact of including data centers in the national carbon emission trading market from a
policy mechanism perspective, providing a new persp ective on carbon asset management for data centers.

2 Assumptions and Justifications
1．Assumption 1: Energy consumption is linearly proportional to HPC computational load
High-performance computing systems typically adju st resource usage based on computational demand.
Assuming a linear relationship between energy consum ption and computational load simplifies the analysis
and provides a foundation for more complex, non-linear models. This assumption allows the total energy
demand of HPC to be estimated using data on full capacity and average utilization.
2．Assumption 2: Energy mix remains relatively stable in the short term
In many regions, the composition of energy production (e.g., the proportion of fossil fuels versus
renewable energy) changes gradually. Assuming a sta ble energy mix in the short term provides clear input

serving as a baseline for future scenario predictions.
3．Assumption 3: Hardware and cooling system efficiencies are constant within model’s scope
Although hardware and cooling system efficiencies may improve over time, their rates of improvement

4．Assumption 4: Carbon emission factors are directly related to the type of energy used
Each energy source (e.g., coal, natural gas, solar, wind) has a specific carbon emission factor, which is
derived from empirical data and research. Assuming a d irect relationship betw een carbon emission factors
and energy types provides a clear rule for estimating th e total carbon emissions of HPC systems based on the
energy mix used.

We then need to clarify the scope of the problem as shown below:
1．Definition of Annual Energy Consumption: The total amount of electrical energy consumed by
the HPC system over the course of a full year (365 days) of operation, usually in megawatt-hours (MWh).
2．Two modes of operation: The energy consumption of the HPC system needs to be calculated
separately for the following two scenarios:
 Full load operation: The situation where the H PC system is continuously running at maximum
power consumption (theoretical upper limit).
 Actual operation (average utilization): The HPC system operates according to actual task
requirements, usually reflecting its actual load in the form of average utilization.

CPUs and GPUs), storage devices, network facilities , and cooling systems. In practice, the load of HPC is
affected by various factors such as work tasks, time c ycles (e.g., peaks and troughs), equipment maintenance,
etc., so the utilization rate is usually lower than the full-load state. Then this paper specifically defines the
annual energy consumption of full-load operation as follows:

10.1 Strengths

environmental impact of HPC systems, including electricity, water resources, chemical fuels, building
energy, equipment manufacturing and waste treatment.

5．Policy recommendations: Based on the model results, technical and policy-oriented

10.2 Weaknesses

the linear relationship between energy consumption and HPC computing load, which may not fully capture
all complex nonlinear dynamics.
2． Data limitations: The model relies on existing data and literature, and there may be problems with
incomplete or outdated data, which affects the accuracy of the prediction.
11 Conclusion
In this study, we conducted an in-depth analysis of the energy consumption and environmental impacts
of high-performance computing ( HPC ) systems by constructing a comprehensive model. The model not
only quantifies the annual energy consumption of globa l HPC systems under different operating conditions,
