Summary Sheet

In this report, we explore how to create an optimal music tour plan for Taylor Swift, a pop musical
performer very well known by young people all ove r the world. First, we collected relevant
information about her past tours including cities visited, tour duration, number of shows, venues,
ticket prices, and popularity in the regions visited. Acknowledging the complexity of the planning
problem, a series of assumptions are required to be made. After listing our assumptions based on
our analysis of the past tours, we defined the factors that shoul d be considered to measure the
success of a tour. We found a Performance Index ( PI) that took into acc ount the total profit,
attendance, and types of cities covered by the tour, and can serve this purpose.
During our planning, we emphasized choosing cities by creating a candidate list that included cities
from different continents. We included many cities that Taylor Swift has visited before, and cities
that she is currently popular in, based on the heat map in [7]. By using our model, we evaluated 6
example plans, each of which picked a different co mbination of cities from the candidate list. In
addition, we calculated the PI of the Reputation Stadium Tour as a comparison to our 6 plans.
The results indicate that by optimizing factors such as city diversification, ticket prices, and venue
capacities, our example plans are more successful compared to the Reputation Stadium Tour. Plan
1 and Plan 2 focused on English-speaking countr ies only, and with optimized ticket prices and
greater venue capacities, they can generate a higher revenue, and thus a larger PI value. Plan 3 to
Plan 6, on the contrary, include various non-English speaking cities (Nc_em) and new cities (Nc_new).
Depending on the values of Nc_em and Nc_new, Plan 3 to Plan 6 get a certain amount of reward,
according to our model of PI, thus these plans are deemed more  successful than Plan 1, Plan 2,
and the Reputation Stadium Tour. Because Plan 6 has the greatest number of Nc_em, our model
appreciates Plan 6 the most, ev en though it has a 5% revenue reduc tion compared to the highest
amount.
Next, we discussed the strengths and weaknesses of our model and wrote a letter to Taylor Swift
presenting our plan to her. Our letter demonstrates our model and persuades her to use our model
for her next tour planning to have higher profit and gain more popularity in diversified areas.

A musical performer has approached our team at the MidMCM Music Agency to ensure that with
our help planning, they can set up the best tour possible to meet their goals. We need to analyze
the performer’s past tours and develop a model to  plan their next one. The model should reflect

include venues, ticket prices, and the number of fans attending each show. After creating the model,

Assumptions below:
1. Assumption: Taylor Swift’s goals for this tour include maximizing profit and public
figure/popularity.
Justification: Our model assumes that the optimization goal of this tour is to maximize
profit and Taylor Swift’s public figure. Based on her explanations for starting past tours,
we know Taylor Swift usually starts tours to  increase her popularity and meet with fans
around the world. The trend we observed also clearly supports this assumption.
2. Assumption: The number of tickets sold in each  concert will be a constant percentage of
the venue capacity, and the constant we used is 78.41%.
Justification: Concert tickets are sold out very early once available to the public, and there
are certain numbers of seats reserved not for sale. The average attendance of each concert
in the Reputation tour is 78.41% [8][9][10][11][12][14].
3. Assumption: Slightly adjusting the ticket price won’t influence the attendance.
Justification: Many Taylor Swift fans try extr emely hard to get tickets for her concerts
before they sell out, as she is  really popular, and the tickets sell out quickly. Therefore, a
fan wouldn’t not purchase the ticket just because of a slight increase in the price. In addition,
even if some fans decide not to purchase a ticket because of changes in the price, Taylor
Swift’s concerts have waitlists of up  to 2 Million peopl e waiting for tickets  [15] , so we
assume that her concerts will always be full and adjusting the ticket price slightly won’t
change the attendance.
4. Assumption: Lodging and transportation costs ca n be ignored, and crew costs and venue
costs don’t have fixed prices, but rather cut off a percentage of the total revenue, so the cost
doesn’t impact the final optimization result when different models are compared.
Justification: When Taylor Swift performs a show, she has to pay for many different things.
Renting a venue and paying her crew make up a large portion of this total cost, which may
be contracted as a percentage of the total revenue, whereas lodging and transportation costs
are very little in comparison. Using The Eras Tour as an example, the total transportation
cost of production equipment, stage, lighting, costumes, instruments and everything else is
around $15M USD, which is 1.5% of the total revenue [16].
