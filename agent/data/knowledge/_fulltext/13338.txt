Executive Summary
Theme parks are commonplace in our twenty-first
century modern world. However, many of us are
unsure of how to make the best use of our time at a
theme park. There are those amongst us who prefer
to be impulsive and  try out everything rather than
plan out activities beforehand. This can often lead
to a poor and unfulfilling experience . To mitigate
against this, MidMCM Travel Agency can plan out
a range of activities for customers; this way
customers will save time on researching the various
activities yet still maximis e their level of
enjoyment. With this in mind,  Ming, Ishmael,
Karine, and Freya hired MidMCM team  to plan
their activities at Polygon Paradise Park.
Firstly, we planned the activity for each of the
friends individually, based on their preferences and
requirements. The Gantt chart is a powerful tool in
project planning and management. In this project,
we have made use of Excel to create the Gantt
charts. The horizontal timeline includes the time of
events being scheduled, ranging from 9am to 9pm.
The other axis contains the name of the events that
take place. To make it easier to understand, arrows
are drawn to show the sequence of the tasks. The
Gantt chart is also colour-coded: red cells represent
tasks that must be finished  within the spe cified
timeframe while green cells are optional and can
occur anywhere between a period . Before we
design the Gantt chart, we first need to gather data.
The data then can be extracted and represented onto
a flow chart. Then, following the steps , and
considering variables such as walking time from
ride to ride ; duration of each activity and their
waiting time, the Gantt chart can be created.
Secondly, to gauge how much the group of friends
enjoyed their day at the theme park, we developed
a happiness mathematical model to calculate the
“Happiness score”. We estimated how much each
friend would enjoy each activity and multiplied this
by the duration of the  activity. Whichever score is
higher will be an indication of which schedule is
the more successful  one. Thus, we designed 9
scheduling cases in total, for example: one group of
4 friends, 2 groups of 2 friends, 2 friends combined
in groups etc. According to the happiness
mathematical model analysis, case 6 appears to be
the best possible schedule; this schedule should be
enjoyed by all friends , in this case, the group of
friends only spilt up for 4 hrs of the day. The basics
of the Gantt chart still apply, but in this case, there
will be two charts with events happening
simultaneously from both groups. However, we
discovered that some the friends’ requirements had
to be compromised. Through this we concluded
that separating the friends is crucial in maximising
enjoyment for the  whole group as the Happiness
score is higher in the schedule with includes 4 hrs
of division into subgroups than the entire 12 hrs
duration.
Finally, we came up with the best route to
maximise the enjoyment level of the group of
friends, which is the optimal Gantt chart providing
them with the highest enjoyment level. However, it
is not possible for them to all their individual
desires to be met by staying together form 9 am to
9pm, at some point during the day, they will have
to split up in order to satisfy more requirements and
maximise enjoyment.  One detailed flow chart
about the trip was also created for better
visualization purposes.
With the increasing popularity of travel agencies
providing this service , we recognise out level of
accuracy will need to be very high and incorporates
as many of the requirements and needs as possible
of the individual within group;  we need also to
legislate for unexpected events.

2.3 Assumptions
Global assumptions
1. The friends' happiness can be evaluated by activity time, walking time, etc.
2. The group of friends enjoy activities such as having lunch and dinner and using the WC.
3. They cannot walk through each of the rides  as we took the rides drawn in the map to be solid. For
example, you cannot walk through the games stall.
4. The friends will enjoy the time devoted to activities more than the buffer time and they will prefer the
waiting time over the walking time

Local assumptions
1. Approximate walking speed is 1.3m/s
Justification: Since the average walking speed of a human is 1.2~1.4m/ s [2], we took the average of that
number, 1.3m/s for the calculation of the time taken to move from each ride.
2. Time for toilet is about 10 to 20 mins
Justification: We have decided that the friends would go to the toilet during the time that they are eating, or
also when there is extra buffer time. We assume that the friends take a maximum of 20 mins and a minimum
of 5 mins to use the toilet. We did not consider the toilet waiting times.
3. Waiting time for the schedule is approximate and there is no change in an hour (such as 9a.m. to 10a.m.)
Justification: Although the chart given is not flexible, we have buffer time in case there is more waitin g time
than expected.
4. Play duration time is fixed for every activity
In this project we define buffer time differently. Buffer time refers to the room for error in the event of
unexpected situations. If at the end of a certain activity, there is a surplus of allocated buffer time , the group
of friends can decided how best to use it. For example, taking a group picture or going on a ride that they all
enjoyed.
