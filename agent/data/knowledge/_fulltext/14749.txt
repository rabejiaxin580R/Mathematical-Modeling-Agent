2. Introduction
2.1 Background
The Summer Olympic Games, held once every four years, is one of the most prestigious
miscellaneous sporting events globally. The Olympics promotes the convergence of nations
through sports, wherein each participating country sends their most prominent athletes to
compete in a diverse array of events. The organizational, managerial, and regulatory aspects are
entrusted to the International Olympic Committee (IOC). [1]. The Olympics originated in ancient
Greece, and the ancient Olympic Games were held in the city-state Olympia from 776 BCE to
393 CE, making the games one of the oldest documented sporting events in history.
The Olympics, with its long history and worldwide acclaim, captivates the attention of an
immense global audience. Data from Statista reveals that the average aggregate viewership for
the preceding four Olympic Games approximated a staggering 3.34 billion individuals [4]. But the
significance of the Olympics is substantially more than simply a source of entertainment. It
functions as a platform wherein athletes can exhibit their aptitude and commitment on an
international stage. Furthermore, the Games provide athletes with an opportunity to congregate
under their respective flags and evokes a sense of pride and patriotism.
This problem is important because if we do not calculate the number of medals needed correctly,
then the IOC will face embarrassment and questions about its credibility, which we need to
prevent. The Olympics also loses its prestige as one of the largest global sporting events, and the
host country (in this case, Paris) is likely to have to take on the burden of being “irresponsible”.

4.4 Medal Prices
In this section, we will look through the prices to produce each type of medal, then multiply
them by 108 (as we will need 108 of each medal type), and finally add them all together to find
the total cost of medals. All material prices in this section are as of November 14, 2023.
First, we will calculate the price of gold medals. Gold medals are required to have at least 92.5%
silver, and usually weigh 556 grams [9] [10]. Gold medals have a gold plating on the outside
consisting of at least 6 grams of pure gold. Thus, the cost of gold in each medal can be found by
6 grams × $62.57/g* = $375.42
*price of gold (per gram)[11]
The cost of silver in each medal can be calculated by (Total weight - Weight of gold) × $0.71/g
(price of silver per gram). The weight of silver in each gold medal is the total weight subtracted
by the weight of gold, which in this case, is 556 grams - 6 grams, or 550 grams per medal. Thus,
the cost of silver in each gold medal is 550 grams × $0.71/g = $390.50.
Then, we can calculate the total cost per gold medal by adding these two together, to get a total
of $765.92 per gold medal. Therefore, the cost of 108 gold medals (the amount required for the
arena) would be (108 × $765.92), or $82,719.36 in total.
Now, we will look into silver medals. Silver medals are made entirely of pure silver. Therefore,
the cost of a whole silver medal would be simply the total weight (550g)[9] × $0.71/g, which
would be $390.50. Therefore, the cost of 108 silver medals is (108 × $390.50), or $42,084.00 in
total.
Moving on, we will investigate bronze medals. Bronze medals are composed of 95% copper and
5% zinc. Currently, zinc costs approximately $0.0026/g, and copper costs $0.23/g[12] . The
weight of copper in each bronze medal is the total weight (450g) × 0.95, which is 427.5 grams.
Multiply that by $0.23/g, and we get that the cost of copper in each bronze medal is $98.33. The
